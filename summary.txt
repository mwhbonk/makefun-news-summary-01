World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026

The United States signalled its confrontation with Iran was easing: Secretary of State Marco Rubio said there was no need to attack Iran again, a day after President Trump called the war "effectively over", while a US maritime blockade continued and oil prices fell to two-month lows (Arab News).

US prosecutors charged three Iranian-linked men, including Iraqi national Mohamed Bakar Saad Dawood, with plotting an attack on RAF Fairford airbase in Britain; Dawood is accused of passing coded messages from jail, and Iran denies involvement (Japan Times).

US, Russian and Ukrainian negotiators resumed trilateral peace talks in Abu Dhabi for a second day, with little progress after four hours on Tuesday; President Zelenskyy called the first day "constructive", and US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are mediating (Le Monde, CNN).

Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang admitted an affair with a woman who may have been a Chinese spy; the FBI is examining the matter, Nvidia hired a former US attorney to investigate, its shares fell, and shareholders sued (Japan Times, BBC).

World leaders began speaking at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey (AFP). In Bangladesh, a shutdown called by the banned Chhatra League brought arson and bomb blasts, killing a bus driver (SMW News).
